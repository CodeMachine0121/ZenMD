A folder makes you decide where something belongs **before you write it down**.

At that moment you do not yet know what you are writing.

## You find out three months later

A note is one line jotted down today and a finished piece three months from now. There is no moment in between where you could say it stopped being a note and became an article.

I tried building folders for this many times. `drafts/`, `ideas/`, `to-sort/` — and every one of them turned into the same place: the one I never open again.

The problem is not the folder. It is that **filing asks for a decision too early**. Sorting is something you do looking back; writing is something you do in the moment. Put the looking back inside the moment and the cost is that you stop to decide where this goes — and in that second, the sentence you were about to write is gone.

## So now I do two things

Write it down, and be able to find it later.

The first has to be fast enough that there is no friction — open, start typing, and nothing in between asks me a question. The second I hand entirely to search, not to structure.

That sounds like giving up on organising. It is not. **It only moves the organising from before you write to the moment you need it.**

## What about tags

I still use tags, but differently: they are not categories, they are **clues**. I no longer ask which pile this belongs in. I ask what words I will reach for in three months when I go looking.

Those two questions sound alike. The answers rarely are.
